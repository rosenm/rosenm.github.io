# Awards and Honors

## Carl Storm Opportunity Fellowship

The [Carl Storm Opportunity Fellowship](https://www.grc.org/about/funding-programs-and-initiatives/) is an award that provides full registration support for one first-time attendee per conference, named in honor of Dr. Carl Storm, Director Emeritus of Gordon Research Conferences (GRC).

I was selected by the conference chairs as the one Neurobiology of Brain Disorders Gordon Research Conference attendee to receive the Carl Storm Opportunity Fellowship. 

---

## Visiting Scholar Award

The [Parkinson's Foundation](https://www.parkinson.org/) Visiting Scholar Award gives graduate students or postdoctoral fellows a $4,000 stipend to visit and work in an outside host laboratory to learn new research techniques that they can apply to their Parkinson's disease (PD) research. Applications are reviewed and selected by scientific experts, including members of the Parkinson’s Foundation Scientific Advisory Board and trained research advocates.

I was awarded the Visiting Scholar Award to perform research with Dr. [Soumya Raychaudhuri](https://scholar.google.com/citations?user=Tntl6sYAAAAJ&hl=en) and his lab at Harvard Medical School/Brigham and Women's Hospital/Broad Institute in Boston, MA. During my time as a visiting scholar, I broadly explored the relationship between known risk/protective human leukocyte antigen (HLA) alleles and T-cell activity in PD patients by relating HLA alleles with T-cell gene expression and receptor characteristics.

The Raychaudhuri lab are experts in statistical genetics, HLA genetics and computational analysis of sequencing data. Working with them, provided invaluable mentorship and taught me about how methods used to study autoimmunity can be utilized in neurodegenerative research.

## Richmond Science Scholar

I was awarded the Richmond Science Scholarship as an incoming student at the University of Richmond and this award covered my entire tuition for all four years of my undergraduate education. 

The University of Richmond awards various merit-based scholarships the **most prestigious and competitive** of which being the [Richmond Scholars Program](https://scholars.richmond.edu/about/program-history.html). These scholarships are equal to full tuition for up to eight consecutive semesters of full-time enrollment including study abroad, housing and food, (in addition to other program benefits) and is given to **approximately 25 incoming students** per year. When I was a part of the Richmond Scholars Program, there were four designations focused on different disciplines including Science Scholars, Boatwright Scholars, Oldham Scholars, and Artist Scholars. Since then, the four designations have been unified to a singular Richmond Scholar community which includes students across academic disciplines and interest.
